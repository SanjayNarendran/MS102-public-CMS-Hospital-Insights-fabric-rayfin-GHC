Hospital quality, safety and access to maternity care across the U.S.

How infections, patient experience, patient safety and outcomes have moved across America’s hospitals from 2019 to 2025, and how far women live from a hospital that delivers babies. The dashboard brings together CMS public reporting for hospitals: CDC standardized infection ratios, HCAHPS patient experience and the PSI‑90 safety composite, plus CMS star ratings, death and readmission rates, Medicare penalties and maternity care data, all built from public CMS and Census files.

    How it runs on Microsoft Fabric

    The dashboard is a static web app. It runs from local files, and can optionally keep its data in Fabric and be hosted by Fabric.

    Semantic model CMS
    Tables HAI, HCAHPS and PSI90 (infections, patient experience and patient safety) in the lakehouse lh_cms_core, served through Direct Lake and queried with DAX (optional: the same numbers can be built locally)
    Semantic model CMS V2
    Outcomes and maternity tables in the lakehouse lh_cms_v2, served through Direct Lake
    Fabric App
    This dashboard, hosted with Rayfin static hosting; viewers sign in with their Fabric account
    Ask your data
    GitHub Copilot, running in Azure Container Apps on each viewer’s own Copilot plan after a GitHub sign-in. Its DAX queries on CMS and CMS V2 run in the app’s Rayfin Functions
    Live services
    Map tiles, road routing and address lookup are fetched while you use the maps; your location stays in the browser

      Standardized Infection Ratios (SIR) compare observed infections with the number predicted from the 2015 national baseline, so below 1.0 is better. Device-associated infections spiked as COVID‑19 filled ICUs, and MRSA bloodstream infections briefly exceeded the baseline at in . Central‑line, catheter and C. diff infections have since reached new lows. Surgical‑site infections have not.

      A raw SIR can mislead: a small hospital with a single infection can post an SIR of 3.0 by chance. The funnel plot sets every hospital against the range expected for its size, so only points outside the funnel are real signals. In the latest report, hospital‑infection pairs were significantly worse than benchmark, down from in .

      Fig 4

      Hospitals outside the funnel have more or fewer infections than chance explains, 2025 report

      Each dot is a hospital. Its infection ratio is the infections it had divided by the number predicted for its size and patients, so 1.0 means as many as predicted. A hospital that expects few infections can land far from 1.0 by chance, because one infection more or less moves its ratio a lot. The shaded funnel shows how far chance alone can take a hospital of each size, so it is wide for small hospitals on the left and narrows for large ones on the right. Dots above it had more infections than chance explains, and dots below it fewer. Colour is CMS’s own verdict for each hospital.

            About the data

            Source: CMS Care Compare hospital data from CMS’s public archive, as tables HAI, HCAHPS and PSI90 (one row per hospital and report year) built by build_core_tables.py, either read directly or queried with DAX from a Fabric semantic model CMS. For demonstration only: every figure depends on the source data.

            The outcomes section (overall star rating, mortality, readmissions, emergency department times and Medicare penalties) and the maternity section use CMS Care Compare, Provider of Services and cost‑report files plus U.S. Census data, built locally by build_quality_extra.py and build_maternity.py. They can also be published to the Fabric semantic model CMS V2 (lakehouse lh_cms_v2) by publish_fabric.py.

            Method notes

            • National and state SIRs are pooled: Σ observed ÷ Σ predicted infections across reporting hospitals.
            • “Better or worse than benchmark” compares each hospital’s 95% confidence interval with SIR 1.0 (2015 baseline).
            • Funnel limits use exact Poisson quantiles with Spiegelhalter interpolation.
            • HCAHPS 2025 omits staff responsiveness and care transition; PSI hip‑fracture and haemorrhage measures changed definitions and are excluded from trend comparisons.
